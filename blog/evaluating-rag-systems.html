How to actually evaluate a RAG system (Recall@k, Hit-Rate@k, MRR)

By Edith Njoroge · Software Engineer & AI Domain Expert · 7 min read · rag-llm-eval repo

Retrieval-augmented generation feels great in a demo: you ask a question, it streams a sourced answer. But how do you know retrieval is actually pulling the right documents, and not just producing a well-written answer to a question it got the evidence for by luck? You measure it. The three metrics that matter most are retrieval-focused, because generation quality is capped by retrieval quality.

Recall@k — did we fetch any of the right documents?

For each question there are ground-truth documents. Recall@k asks: of those correct documents, how many landed in the top k retrieved? High recall means the evidence is in front of the model.

Hit-Rate@k — did the right thing appear at all?

Hit-rate@k is the binary cousin: for each query, did the top k contain at least one relevant document? (1 if yes, 0 if no, averaged across the set.) It is coarse but instantly readable — “85% of queries got relevant evidence in the top 5.”

MRR — how high did the right answer rank?

Mean Reciprocal Rank is sensitive to position: a relevant document at rank 1 scores 1.0, at rank 2 scores 0.5, at rank 3 scores 0.33, and so on. MRR rewards pipelines that put the best evidence at the top, which is what a high-quality answer depends on. If your MRR is low but recall is high, the evidence is there but your ranking is bad — a different failure than “we’re not retrieving it at all.”

Why the harness is the hard part

The metrics are easy. The hard part is reproducibility: a fixed evaluation set, deterministic chunking and embedding, and an automated script that recomputes scores so you can compare changes. My rag-llm-eval repo builds the chain from scratch (chunking, embedding, retrieval) with a harness that computes Recall@k, Hit-Rate@k, and MRR on a small ground-truth set — in pure local Python, no API keys, so it is verifiable end to end.

The takeaway: domains matter. Evaluators and engineers get paid to make “good” measurable. If you can turn a vague “seems right” into Recall@5 = 0.82, MRR = 0.61, you have the skill that both AI-evaluation platforms and engineering teams actually hire for.
